# Body dimensions versus stature: measured human data

For a direct comparison of static versus changing dimensions, child/adult height bins, additional head-age data and an explicitly flagged 100–250 cm model grid, see [HEIGHT_VARIATION_REPORT.md](HEIGHT_VARIATION_REPORT.md).

This is research data, **not game-derived proportions**. It supplements, and does not replace, the skeletal bone-ratio TSV one directory above. Surface lengths include skin and soft tissue and cannot be substituted for osteometric bone lengths. These are population associations, not measurements of either CT subject, and are not clinical normal ranges.

## Deliverables

* `HEIGHT_FORWARD_REGRESSIONS.tsv`: 76 sex-specific **forward** ordinary least-squares models, `dimension_cm = intercept_cm + slope * stature_cm`. Each row identifies source, sex, dimension, paired N, observed stature range, means, slope and its 95% conventional t interval, R², residual SD, residual 2.5th/97.5th percentiles and the mean and sample SD of each person's measured dimension/stature ratio. The ratio at a particular height on the fitted line is `slope + intercept_cm / stature_cm`; it is **not** necessarily the reported mean of observed ratios. Use actual paired records if a nonlinear curve is wanted.
* `PAIRED_RATIOS_VS_HEIGHT.tsv`: 26 additional sex-specific fits of observed **individual ratios** to stature. `ratio_intercept + ratio_slope_per_cm * stature_cm` estimates the mean ratio over the cohort's observed height range; numerator and denominator identify each ratio. `ratio_mean` and `ratio_sd` are individual ratios, not a ratio of separate group means. Surface-landmark ratios are not ratios of bone lengths. This direct regression of the ratio is not the same model as dividing two independently fitted dimension regressions.
* `ANSUR_II_PAIRED_MM.csv`: all 6,068 public ANSUR II participants, with age, sex and 92 measured body dimensions plus five explicitly named derived dimensions. Unit is mm, including `interpupillarybreadth`, converted from the source's tenths of a millimetre; source weight in tenths of a kilogram is omitted. Public `SubjectId` is retained for cross-referencing, not a name. The two 17-year-olds remain in the raw data but not the adult fits.
* `ANSUR_HEIGHT_BINS.tsv`: observed means/SDs of selected dimensions and individual ratios in sex-specific 5 cm stature bins; lower boundary inclusive, upper exclusive. A bin with one person has no SD (`NA`); tiny bins are not reliable population estimates.
* `NHANES_2017_2018_PAIRED_CM.csv`: 7,697 public health-survey records, age ≥2, with measured standing height and at least one arm/leg length. Values are cm. `RIAGENDR` 1=male, 2=female; `RIDAGEYR` is age in years at interview (80 means 80 or more), `RIDEXAGM` age in months at exam when released. `SEQN` is the public survey identifier. `BMXHT`, `BMXARML`, `BMXLEG` are standing height, upper-arm length and upper-leg length. Blank measurement means missing, never zero. `BMIHT`, `BMIARML`, `BMILEG` are measurement comments; 1 means could not obtain, and BMIHT 3 means not straight. `WTMEC2YR`, `SDMVSTRA`, `SDMVPSU` are examination weights and masked strata/PSU for survey analysis. Arm/leg lengths are usually right side, otherwise left where right could not be measured; the public file does not identify side in that case.

### Interpretation of fits

ANSUR adult models use age ≥18: 4,081 male and 1,985 female; the adult stature ranges are 149.1–199.3 cm and 140.9–182.9 cm respectively. ANSUR is a US Army **anthropometric/ergonomic** survey of selected military personnel, not a clinical or globally representative sample. The larger clinical source is the CDC NHANES health examination, covering civilians and both adults and children. The NHANES models use ≥18-year-olds, paired nonmissing height/dimension and exclude records with a height or relevant dimension comment. They are deliberately **unweighted descriptive fits**, not US population estimates: CDC instructs use of the examination weights and complex sample design. The raw file supplies weights/strata/PSU for an appropriately survey-weighted reanalysis. The survey has no medical exclusions for the body measurements protocol; no assumption of disease-free subjects is made.

For both sources, slope confidence intervals assume an independent, homoscedastic linear model; Army sample design and NHANES clustering/weights are **not** accounted for. Residual SD is `sqrt(SSE/(n-2))`, not error of the estimated mean or an individual prediction interval. Residual percentiles describe in-sample errors and are not a guaranteed 95% interval at each height. All quantities, especially at height extremes with small counts, have sampling and model uncertainty; R² does not establish causation or normality. Separate equations fitted independently need not produce a geometrically consistent body and residuals are correlated. Do not sum endpoint measurements unless their landmark definitions match.

### Landmarks and units

Sources: [official ANSUR II database and variable list](https://ph.health.mil/topics/workplacehealth/ergo/Pages/Anthropometric-Database.aspx), [Gordon et al. 2012 survey technical report, NATICK/TR-15/007](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA611869.pdf), and the [anthropometric definitions manual](https://mreed.umtri.umich.edu/mreed/downloads/anthro/ansur/ADAS-Dimension_Definitions.pdf). Definitions in this general manual identify landmarks; the ANSUR II technical report is authoritative for its exact protocol. [CDC NHANES 2017 protocol and codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/BMX_J.htm) and [2017 procedures manual](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes/public/2017/manuals/2017_Anthropometry_Procedures_Manual.pdf); also [clinical anthropometry procedures](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK537315/).

* `sittingheight`: seated vertex-to-seat includes head and neck, **not torso-only length**. `subischialheight = stature - sittingheight` is a standard indirect leg proxy, not actual summed femur/tibia length or crotch height.
* `acromionradialelength`: acromion (shoulder tip) to radiale (elbow); `radialestylionlength`: radiale to wrist stylion. These are surface landmark measurements, **not** humerus and radius lengths. NHANES `BMXARML` ends at **olecranon**, so its coefficient should not be treated as interchangeable with acromion–radiale.
* `forearmhandlength` is an elbow-to-fingertip surface measurement; `span` is both arms outstretched across the body, not one arm. `handlength`, `palmlength` and `footlength` are separate surface dimensions.
* `trochanterionheight`, `tibialheight`, `lateralmalleolusheight` are vertical distances from the ground to their named surface landmarks. `trochanter_to_tibiale_vertical = trochanterionheight - tibialheight`; `tibiale_to_malleolus_vertical = tibialheight - lateralmalleolusheight`. They are vertical differences between differently placed landmarks, not bone lengths or joint-centre distances. `functionalleglength` is heel-to-posterior-waist in a seated extended-leg posture, not anatomical femur+tibia.
* `headlength` is anterior–posterior; `headbreadth` transverse; `headcircumference` is a perimeter. `tragiontopofhead` is ear-to-crown, not the whole head. `mentonsellionlength` is chin-to-nasal-root, not crown-to-chin. `vertex_to_eye_sitting = sittingheight - eyeheightsitting`; `vertex_to_cervicale_vertical = stature - cervicaleheight` includes a neck component. None is a validated measurement of complete vertical crown-to-chin head height. Do not add ear-to-crown to face length as if the endpoints coincide.
* `waistbacklength` and `waistfrontlengthsitting` are protocol-specific skin surface distances, not bare-spine length. Circumferences and breadths in the raw file are not longitudinal measurements; refer to the definitions before fitting.

## Crown-to-chin head height: remaining gap

The ANSUR II and NHANES 2017–2018 public datasets do **not** contain paired crown-to-chin/stature data. A later head-age study and an exploratory Korean-language source are documented in [HEIGHT_VARIATION_REPORT.md](HEIGHT_VARIATION_REPORT.md); the latter lacks a verified primary data dictionary and cannot yet establish a clinical crown-to-chin regression. DINED defines head height as menton-to-vertex perpendicular to the Frankfort plane: [TU Delft measurement definitions](https://dined.io.tudelft.nl/en/database/tool). [Murakami and Otsuka (1957)](https://doi.org/10.3861/jshhe.23.55), a Japanese sample of 1,600 males and 1,600 females aged 4–21, reports mean head heights at age 21 of 23.4 cm male and 22.4 cm female, and adult mean stature/head-height indices of 7.1 and 6.9 respectively. The accessible English abstract does not fully specify the endpoint method, matching age-group denominators, raw pairs, dispersion or stature-conditional slope. These are separate group summaries, **not** a regression or paired data, and should not be spliced into the Army fits as though measured on the same people. Other papers measure auricular height rather than complete head height or predict stature from head measurements: algebraically inverting those regressions does not produce the desired conditional expectation.

## Limits of 1–2.5 m and clinical evidence

Neither adult Army cohort nor the adult NHANES pairs supports a universal adult model at 100 cm or 250 cm. Some NHANES **children** are around 100 cm; those rows are retained with age but are not interchangeable with short adults, and are excluded from adult models. Growth changes proportions with age. [US clinical sitting-height and leg-length growth references](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343465758_Leg_length_and_sitting_height_reference_data_and_charts_for_children_in_the_United_States) use NHANES III and include sex/age/ancestry; they are pediatric, not adult extreme-height curves.

A short adult may be proportionate or have any of several distinct growth conditions. [MedlinePlus achondroplasia](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/achondroplasia/) reports mean adult heights 131 cm male and 124 cm female, with disproportionately short proximal limbs and a relatively average-sized trunk. In one [clinical achondroplasia study](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/spirometry-and-chest-wall-dimensions-in-achondroplasia-3/) adults had mean sitting/standing ratio 0.66; the paper included 44 males and 58 females aged 7–60, but the reported adult ratio is pooled and not a sex-specific height regression. Do not apply it to all short adults. [Additional dysplasia review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8061761/) reports other conditions with different body proportions and adult heights as low as 94 cm; it does not provide paired segment lengths for both sexes.

Near the upper endpoint, [Kara 2020 case report](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1076540) reports a 37-year-old male with pituitary gigantism, a recorded stature of 2.51 m (251 cm), right/left thigh surface measurements 59/62 cm, right/left lower-leg measurements 59/63 cm and right/left feet 35.5/36.5 cm. The article describes the thigh as inguinal ligament to upper patella and the leg as tibial plateau to floor; the right tibia had previously been fractured. It inconsistently labels 2.51 as cm in its table, describes earlier Guinness measurements and provides other implausible/ambiguous entries, so measurement timing and table quality limit use. This is **one clinical case**, not a population curve or a basis for estimating female proportions; do not silently average it with the Army data. [Merck's clinical account](https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/hormonal-and-metabolic-disorders/pituitary-gland-disorders/gigantism-and-acromegaly) distinguishes excessive GH before vs after growth-plate closure. There is insufficient paired medical evidence here for sex-specific adult curves throughout 1–2.5 m. Extrapolation, scaling, or separate condition-specific character designs would be assumptions, not validated medical results.

Mirror is a third-party transcription; the public CDC XPT files are authoritative. Both input files contain additional sensitive survey variables; only the public fields listed above were extracted. Verify against original CDC files before high-stakes use.

With Python, NumPy, pandas and SciPy installed, place the two original Army CSVs in `ANSUR_DIR` and the two mirror files in `NHANES_DIR`, then run:

```
python tools/skeleton-source/openskeleton/18-height-anthropometry.py --ansur-dir ANSUR_DIR --nhanes-dir NHANES_DIR
```

Extraction keeps paired values, including flagged and missing NHANES observations, while fits apply the exclusions above. Regression is calculated directly as `cov(H,Y)/var(H)`; no reversed stature-on-dimension regression is inverted. No runtime/game code is changed.
