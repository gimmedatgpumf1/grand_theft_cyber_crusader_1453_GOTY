# Nonlinear major-body-part proportion curves

These are **exploratory surface-dimension models**, not individual-bone scaling rules. They use paired, sex- and age-grouped real-person measurements instead of assuming that each dimension is a fixed percentage of stature. They do not change the live skeleton, poses, joints or ragdoll. The simple C++ evaluator `native/client/body_proportion_curves.h` is available to the client but is **not wired into skeleton geometry**. `BODY_PART_CURVE_KNOTS.tsv` is the data it reads; `BODY_PART_CURVE_HOLDOUT.tsv` compares prediction error with straight-line and power-law alternatives. No specific scale law is asserted to be universally correct.

## Method

The generator partitions each source by sex and recorded age (`under_18` or `adult_18_plus`); child records begin at 2 years. Age must be selected separately: it cannot be inferred reliably from height. For each body dimension it computes paired-sample mean dimension and mean stature in 5 cm stature bins. A bin becomes a knot only if it contains at least 20 valid pairs. At a supported stature the C++ evaluator uses shape-preserving piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation (PCHIP), with no extrapolation. PCHIP passes through the recorded knot means and avoids overshoot within monotonic knot intervals; it does **not** force globally monotonic growth or prove that a cubic is a biological law. Gaps over 10 cm between knot centres return no value; exact knots remain evaluable. A single knot is only available at that exact height. Knot positions are means, not bin edges. None of the models supplies usable predictions near 250 cm; a missing result is not a zero or a reason to silently apply an unvalidated extrapolation.

The `height_minus_sitting` dimension is computed as stature minus sitting height, including between knots, so those two values always sum to stature. It is a derived **surface distance**, not hip-to-floor or a sum of thigh and shin bone lengths. Sitting height includes head and neck, not just torso. Do not independently add it to separate head height, or treat any of these surface dimensions as fitted joint-centre-to-joint-centre distances. NHANES III upper arm and ANSUR II upper arm use different surface landmarks; the two pelvis breadth measures also differ. Keep source and dimension together rather than combining them as identical measurements. ANSUR `thigh_vertical` and `lower_leg_vertical` are differences between ground-to-surface-landmark heights, not bone or joint distances. `ear_to_crown` is not total head height; `head_depth` is front-to-back.

## Sources and population limits

- **NHANES III**: U.S. 1988–94 civilian examined sample, stature with sitting height, upper arm, upper leg, shoulder and iliac surface breadth. The original file supplies complex-survey weights, but these exploratory bins are **unweighted**. Height flags and missing values were handled during extraction, not all possible covariates or survey design. See [SCALING_DATA_RESEARCH.md](SCALING_DATA_RESEARCH.md) and the [NHANES III anthropometry manual](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes3/manuals/anthro.pdf).
- **ANSUR II**: adult U.S. Army personnel, not a general population. Surface head breadth/depth, ear-to-crown, arms, hands, feet, shoulder/pelvis breadth and vertical leg landmarks, by recorded sex. See [README.md](README.md) for definitions and source data.
- **AnthroKids 1977 excerpt**: historic U.S. children and young adults, paired stature and heel-to-longest-toe foot length. Only this explicitly documented foot field is used; see [SCALING_DATA_RESEARCH.md](SCALING_DATA_RESEARCH.md). No child hand or complete head-height curve is invented.

Sources describe different populations, eras, measurement procedures and incomplete individual samples. These are cross-sectional *conditional sample averages*, not growth trajectories, clinical reference ranges or an individual prediction. Adult and child models may disagree at the same height and must not be silently spliced; age, developmental status and condition affect proportions. Some bins have far more subjects than others. Every emitted knot retains `n` and source. SD or prediction intervals are not provided by the evaluator; an average does not describe individual variation. Broad source-specific age groups remain confounded with height. Avoid using `2.5 m` extrapolations as medically supported data.

## What the comparison shows

For each source, one seeded approximately 80/20 identifier-level split is shared across dimensions. On the training data we fit three forward dimension-on-stature models: linear, power-law and PCHIP through the qualifying 5 cm training-bin means. The test is restricted to subjects supported by the training PCHIP and compares all models on the same subjects; `n_test_supported` and `total_test` show excluded edge/gap cases. It is **one internal random holdout**, not independent population validation, an uncertainty interval or proof that a method generalizes. The AnthroKids excerpt has one exact duplicate row, removed here; seven other repeated IDs have conflicting records and cannot safely be assumed to identify the same person. We retain those records but conservatively assign records sharing an ID to the same split. Full-data knots in the table are not the held-out training knots. Source samples overlap in different dimensions, so wins are correlated and not independent experiments.

Example: male NHANES III under-18 sitting height has test MAE **1.655 cm linear**, **1.720 cm power**, and **1.585 cm PCHIP**, on 960 supported test subjects. Some adult ANSUR dimensions are equally well or better described by linear or power models; the comparison does not justify imposing a universal curve. Using the full-data PCHIP, estimated male under-18 sitting height is about **55.05 cm at 100 cm stature** and **89.40 cm at 175 cm** (approximately 55.0% and 51.1% of stature). Those are different people, not an individual's growth. Such curved group relationships are precisely why fixed per-part percentages or a single linear formula should not be assumed.

## Reproduction and use

`25-body-part-curves.py` reads the repository's paired NHANES III and ANSUR II CSV files plus the pinned AnthroKids excerpt (`anthrokids.csv`, SHA-256 `ece6ccb1486915329c8b9f651b6ef21d9972cacfc5c4eae8fdac241fabed3a50`). All three hashes are checked. With `numpy`, `pandas` and `scipy` installed:

```
python3 tools/skeleton-source/openskeleton/25-body-part-curves.py /path/to/anthrokids.csv
```

In C++, load the knot table once, select an exact `{source, sex, ageGroup, dimension}` key from the TSV, and call `dimensionCm(key, statureCm)`. It returns an optional dimension in cm; absence is explicit. Failed reloads leave existing data intact. Rebuilding the skeleton requires mapping these surface measurements to this subject's fitted landmarks, keeping original dimensions at the original preset, transforming linked groups coherently, refitting joint/constraint frames, rebuilding collision shapes and mass/inertia, and checking ground contact. That work is **not yet implemented**. Current ratios are not a direct replacement for skeletal scaling factors.
