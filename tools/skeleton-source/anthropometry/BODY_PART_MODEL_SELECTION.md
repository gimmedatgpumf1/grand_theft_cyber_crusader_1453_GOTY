# Which body-part scale fits best, and what happens at 50 cm?

## Selection for each measurement

`BODY_PART_MODEL_SELECTION.tsv` gives the selected model **per source, sex, age group and dimension**, alongside training-set cross-validation MAE, runner-up gap, independent internal-holdout MAE, counts, supported stature range and an explicitly **untrusted** diagnostic at 50 cm. `BODY_PART_MODEL_CV.tsv` lists every candidate, and `BODY_PART_SELECTED_MODELS.tsv` contains the coefficients used by `BodyProportionCurves::selectedDimensionCm`. Group-level choices here are not one globally best biological formula; these samples differ in population, ages and measurement landmarks.

The candidates are linear, quadratic, logarithmic, power-law and the existing shape-preserving piecewise cubic through 5-cm-bin means. The quadratic uses `z = stature_cm - 150`; logarithmic uses `log(stature_cm/150)`; power uses `stature_cm/150`. All predict **cm of surface measurement**, not a direct game-bone multiplier. Two identically named dimensions across different sources can have different anatomical endpoints. The fitting procedure keeps the original identifier-level 80/20 train/test partition, assigns training IDs to five folds, compares all candidates on the **same** supported subjects in each fold, chooses the lowest pooled out-of-fold MAE, then evaluates that choice on the untouched test partition. Full-data coefficients are fitted after selection. Repeated IDs in the AnthroKids excerpt remain together; one exact duplicate was removed. Implausible nonpositive or greater-than-stature fitted values on a dense training-support grid disqualify a candidate, and a final check is applied on the full supported grid. Those weak shape checks are not clinical validation. Folds and the test split come from the same study, not independent populations. Differences between methods are frequently tiny; `runner_up_gap_cm` is an error difference, not a confidence interval or evidence that one family is truly superior. No universal relationship is established.

`height_minus_sitting` is derived, not separately fitted: its result equals `stature - selected sitting height` between knots as well. Counts and errors are individual paired observations, not estimates of normative ranges. Survey data are unweighted and ages are broadly grouped. Supported ranges are bounded by bin means and exclude interior gaps over 10 cm, even for linear/power models. Both the PCHIP evaluator and selected evaluator return no value outside that support; no extrapolated height is silently applied.

## 50 cm stress test: not an adult at a smaller scale

**None of the 52 child/adult models is supported at 50 cm.** Applying a family selected on adult or older-child data to a baby is extrapolation across developmental and measurement regimes. The test reveals why guards are necessary: the female adult NHANES selected sitting-height model gives a diagnostic **−1.07 cm**, and its upper-leg model gives **−20.21 cm** at 50 cm. The male ANSUR adult hand model gives −2.20 cm. These numbers are exposed solely in the diagnostic column; `selectedDimensionCm` and `dimensionCm` reject 50 cm rather than returning them. PCHIP has no extrapolated diagnostic. The tests do not prove that all extrapolations are wrong or that all in-range values are anatomically correct.

Actual infant data provide a sanity reference, not an invented fit. [Abdel-Rahman et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5751918/) measured 1,681 preterm/full-term infants in their development set, aged 0–90 days: mean **recumbent length 50.6 cm**, upper arm **9.5 ± 1.2 cm**, ulnar forearm **7.7 ± 1.0 cm**, upper leg **8.3 ± 1.4 cm**, and tibial lower leg **8.2 ± 1.2 cm**. These are sample means, **not conditional means for babies exactly 50 cm**. [The same cohort's PMA-based fitted centiles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/5794488/bin/NIHMS936713-supplement-Supp_Table.docx) at 40 weeks postmenstrual age (228 observations) are tabulated separately, not equated to length-specific centiles. Measurements are tape-measured surface landmarks: shoulder tip–olecranon, ulnar styloid–olecranon, inguinal crease–patella, and medial tibial landmarks; they are not maximum bones or joint-centre distances. They are also not a prescription for a particular sex.

[Aziz et al.](https://www.theprofesional.com/index.php/tpmj/article/download/5238/3566/12766) reported, for 1,028 term Pakistani neonates, pooled crown–heel **50.6 ± 2.1 cm** and crown–rump **34.0 ± 1.5 cm**, with sex means in `NEONATAL_BODY_PART_REFERENCES.tsv`. The rump measure is taken supine with flexed hips; it is not identical to seated height. A [Tanzanian study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2458-10-624) reported first-day foot length **7.8 cm** mean for 529 babies, but did not provide paired stature-conditioned means; it measures heel to big toe, not necessarily longest toe. These independent populations cannot be treated as one measured individual. Head **circumference** is not head height. The neonatal US sample mean head circumference was 35.1 cm, not a 35.1 cm tall head. These are rough plausibility anchors only, not normality cutoffs or verified input values for a 50-cm character.

We also extracted new **NHANES III ages 2–<24 months** recumbent-length/upper-arm and head-circumference bins in `INFANT_NHANES_III_LENGTH_BINS.tsv`. At 50–<55 cm, arm/length data include only **2 males and 7 females**; they are not an adequate newborn model. The survey starts at two months, so it is not a birth reference. The unweighted bins retain potential outliers; for example, singleton reported lengths of 39.1 cm at 3 months and 107 cm at 11 months warrant source/measurement review rather than being treated as normal growth or silently excluded. No subject-level values are redistributed. Other infant head-height, hand, foot, shoulder and pelvis curves do not have adequate paired support here. The data do not yet justify a smooth, validated 50–250 cm formula for each part, and the live skeleton is **not scaled**. Real baby proportions require age/gestational context and a separate validated infant reference, not unchecked extrapolation.

## Reproduce

```
python3 tools/skeleton-source/openskeleton/26-select-body-models.py /path/to/anthrokids.csv
python3 tools/skeleton-source/openskeleton/27-infant-length-bins.py /path/to/exam.jsonl.zst
```

Dependencies are `numpy`, `pandas`, `scipy` and `zstandard`; inputs are pinned by SHA-256. `NEONATAL_BODY_PART_REFERENCES.tsv` is a labelled transcription from the linked studies. Run the 25 script first to regenerate the curve knots. The native selected-model loader must be explicitly given both the knots and model tables. Neither loader is connected to the current skeleton/ragdoll.
